How it works — no DMs, no trust, just code

Each lot is a two-leg atomic swap with one shared secret. The maker locked the EXCAL first, in a QHTLC lock. You lock the BTC second, in a P2WSH HTLC with the same secret hash. He claims your BTC — the secret appears on-chain — and you claim the EXCAL with it. If anything goes wrong, your BTC refunds to you after the BTC timeout; his EXCAL can only refund after the BTC leg has already expired, so he cannot take both.

  1. Build the BTC leg with the self-serve kit. Run from the kit’s self-serve/ directory. The kit’s taker_htlc.py builds your BTC HTLC funding PSBT and adds the 1,000-sat signal plus the OP_RETURN tag the watcher keys on. The wallet’s btc-htlc lock builds the same HTLC but without the signal, so the watcher would never see it — use the kit for this step on these lots:
    python3 taker_htlc.py --lot 1excal --amount 100000000 --utxo TXID:VOUT --refund-pubkey YOUR_COMPRESSED_PUBKEY --change-address bc1q... --btc-height CURRENT_BTC_TIP
    Amounts: 1 EXCAL lot takes exactly 100,000,000 sats; the 1,000 EXCAL lot takes any amount you choose; the 0.001 EXCAL pilot takes exactly 100,000 sats. The HTLC timeout is the BTC tip + 144 blocks (~24h). The kit writes funding.psbt and delivery_key.txt (0600) — keep the delivery key, it decrypts your claim seed in step 5.
  2. Sign, broadcast, and post the witness script. Sign funding.psbt in your own wallet and broadcast it — from your wallet, or with the wallet CLI (from the repo root): python3 -m excalibur_wallet wallet broadcast --tx <signed hex> --network bitcoin --yes (BTC broadcast needs your Bitcoin node RPC.) Then post your funding txid and witness-script hex as a comment on the lot’s GitHub issue, so the watcher can verify your funding. Get the witness script from the wallet with the lot’s secret hash, the maker’s claim pubkey 02e8311342cb7accd4edef31462fcda1ef56bfbf7d4f03d3c8872dbec9a1bba1b3, your refund pubkey, and the timeout: python3 -m excalibur_wallet wallet btc-htlc show --claim-pubkey 02e8311342cb7accd4edef31462fcda1ef56bfbf7d4f03d3c8872dbec9a1bba1b3 --refund-pubkey YOUR_COMPRESSED_PUBKEY --secret-hash <lot secret hash above> --timeout <BTC tip + 144>
  3. Wait for the claim seed. After 6 Bitcoin confirmations the watcher publishes your encrypted claim seed to self-serve/claim-seeds/<lot>-<funding_txid>.json. Only the first verified funding per lot is served.
  4. The maker claims your BTC. He claims with the wallet — python3 -m excalibur_wallet wallet btc-htlc claim --utxo <HTLC TXID:VOUT> --utxo-value <sats> --secret <secret> --dest-address bc1q... --witness-script <hex> — and the secret appears in his claim transaction’s witness.
  5. Decrypt the seed and take the secret. python3 decrypt_seed.py --ciphertext claim-seeds/<lot>-<funding_txid>.json --delivery-key delivery_key.txt Read the secret from the maker’s BTC claim transaction on mempool.space; its witness carries the preimage.
  6. Claim the EXCAL with the kit. The lot’s QHTLC claim witness is a Trifold signature from the maker’s claim seed, which only the watcher delivers — so this step stays in the kit, not the wallet:
    python3 taker_claim.py --lot 1excal --secret REVEALED_SECRET --claim-seed DECRYPTED_SEED --destination 1YOUR_LEGACY_ADDRESS
    Your destination must be a legacy P2PKH address (1...); the fork does not accept SegWit outputs. Broadcast the signed claim with python3 -m excalibur_wallet wallet broadcast --tx <signed hex> --network fork --yes. It can only confirm once the fork is mining again (see the stall note above).
  7. If it goes wrong, refund. If the maker never claims, refund your BTC after the BTC timeout: python3 -m excalibur_wallet wallet btc-htlc refund --utxo <HTLC TXID:VOUT> --utxo-value <sats> --dest-address bc1q... --btc-height CURRENT_BTC_TIP --witness-script <hex> The EXCAL leg refunds to the maker only after its fork timeout — and only after the BTC leg has expired.

Why this is real

  • All three lots are funded on-chain on the Excalibur fork (blocks 42,900, 42,899 and 43,571) — the EXCAL is locked, not promised.
  • QHTLC locks: hash-based, quantum-resistant Trifold signatures, not ECDSA. The claim needs the secret and the maker’s claim seed, delivered encrypted to the taker — neither alone unlocks the lot.
  • Opcode-free locks, AuxPoW merge-mined with Bitcoin. The fork split from Bitcoin at block 968,698.
  • One secret, two legs: the maker can only refund the EXCAL after the BTC leg has already expired, so he cannot take both. Your BTC refunds to you after the BTC timeout (tip + 144).
  • Broadcast is real: the wallet builds, signs and verifies every signature locally, then wallet broadcast submits to the fork or to Bitcoin. Nothing here is a demo.
  • Only the first verified funding per lot is served. The secret is the same for everyone per lot, so the first funded HTLC wins and later fundings refund.

New swaps — the wallet flow, end to end

For a new swap — not the three lots above — the wallet runs the whole thing with HTLC legs on both chains and one shared secret hash. Both sides need an excalibur wallet first (run the commands below from the repo root). The maker creates the swap and the secret; the taker joins with the published secret hash.

  1. Maker creates: python3 -m excalibur_wallet wallet swap create --role maker --counterparty-pubkey TAKER_PUBKEY --excal-amount SWORDS --btc-amount SATS --excal-timeout FORK_H --btc-timeout BTC_H --fork-height CUR_FORK --btc-height CUR_BTC — the secret is generated and shown once, never stored. (Taker side: --role taker --secret-hash <published hash> instead of a secret.)
  2. Maker funds the EXCAL leg: python3 -m excalibur_wallet wallet swap fund-excal --swap-id ID --save excal-fund.json, then python3 -m excalibur_wallet wallet broadcast --file excal-fund.json --network fork --yes.
  3. Taker records it and funds the BTC leg: python3 -m excalibur_wallet wallet swap note-excal-funded --swap-id ID --raw-hex <funding tx>, then python3 -m excalibur_wallet wallet swap fund-btc --swap-id ID --utxo TXID:VOUT --utxo-value SATS --utxo-scriptpubkey HEX --save btc-fund.json and python3 -m excalibur_wallet wallet broadcast --file btc-fund.json --network bitcoin --yes.
  4. Maker claims the BTC, revealing the secret: python3 -m excalibur_wallet wallet swap claim-btc --swap-id ID --secret SECRET --dest-address bc1q... --save btc-claim.json, then broadcast it.
  5. Taker learns the secret and claims the EXCAL: python3 -m excalibur_wallet wallet swap note-btc-claimed --swap-id ID --raw-hex <maker’s claim tx>, then python3 -m excalibur_wallet wallet swap claim-excal --swap-id ID --secret SECRET --dest LOCKHEX --utxo TXID:VOUT --fork-height CUR_FORK, then broadcast it.
  6. Refunds: the taker refunds BTC after its timeout (python3 -m excalibur_wallet wallet swap refund-btc --swap-id ID --dest-address bc1q... --btc-height CUR_BTC); the maker refunds EXCAL only after the fork timeout and after the BTC leg expired (python3 -m excalibur_wallet wallet swap refund-excal --swap-id ID --dest LOCKHEX --utxo TXID:VOUT --fork-height CUR_FORK --btc-height CUR_BTC) — the wallet refuses an EXCAL refund while the BTC leg is still live.

Timeouts: the BTC leg must expire first and the EXCAL leg at least 24 hours later in wall-clock terms (defaults: BTC tip + 144, fork tip + 2880). Check a swap any time with python3 -m excalibur_wallet wallet swap show --swap-id ID.

Messerene / pos28 — the genesis nonce, embedded

The Bitcoin genesis block nonce 2,083,236,893 (0x7C2BAC1D) sits at position 28 of the 32-byte Messerene pattern (…B3C5D7E9 → …1DAC2B7C). Read as a scalar, the embedded pattern is a valid key whose pubkey is the SHA-256 preimage of two addresses at once:

  • P2PKH (base58): 17GHncLkSxQYC5TdfuNxjkjmNb4ZaepawM
  • P2WPKH (bech32): bc1qgj6srdszgygpltt2kc3eqva0alx9g48gj9fyj5

Both are public by construction — a fingerprint, never a wallet. Never fund either address.

Burn addresses — 9,020 sats that can never move

BIP-350 (bech32m) re-encodings of the f-pattern and all-zero programs. Neither 32-byte program is a valid curve point, so neither is a Taproot key at all. The all-zero 32-byte address holds 9,020 sats in 12 confirmed UTXOs, 0 ever spent (verified by both mempool.space and BlockCypher) — real burned coins, provably unspendable because x = 0 is not on secp256k1:

bc1pqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqpqqenm

Every angle was tried and failed with evidence — key-path, script-path, quantum, miner collusion. The only theoretical path is a consensus-rule change: a fork of Bitcoin, not a spend on it.

Every send is build/sign/verify, and wallet broadcast --file/--tx --network fork|bitcoin --yes submits it — the broadcast path is real, added Nov 14. The three lots above are QHTLC locks funded before the swap flow existed, so their EXCAL claim still goes through the claim-seed kit step; new swaps run in the wallet end to end. Linux one-file binary built; the Windows .exe builds on a Windows machine.
  • Light ledger — the fork’s UTXO set as a snapshot committed by two sorted-Merkle roots, Trifold-signed checkpoints, a URUU bond on zkSync Era. Real-chain test passed; contracts compile. Not deployed yet. Trust-minimized, not trust-free.
  • Golden Stone — one legendary coin at fork height 50,000 only, priced at 1 EXCAL for 1 BTC. A 1 EXCAL lock is already confirmed on the fork; the counterparty HTLC lock awaits its taker pubkey.
  • KNOT lock — built, tested 60/60, staged. Activation at 100,000 is a proposal only; nothing broadcast or mined.
