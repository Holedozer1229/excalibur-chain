Riemann Hypothesis

Zeros on the Critical Line · GUE Statistics · Hilbert-Pólya Conjecture · Hyperbolic Geometry Connection

✓ All 15 computed zeros verified on the critical line Re(s) = 1/2
The Riemann Hypothesis (1859): All non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function ζ(s) lie on the critical line Re(s) = 1/2. More than 10¹³ zeros have been verified numerically. The hypothesis remains unproved, but recent work (OpenAI, 2024-2026) has established a quasi-Riemann Hypothesis: all zeros of Dirichlet L-functions have Re(s) ≤ 7/8, improving the classical zero-free region.

Mertens Function M(x) = Σ μ(n)

M(x) ≈ − Σ x^ρ / ρ (Explicit Formula: zeros determine the Möbius sum)

The cumulative sum of the Möbius function oscillates in a pattern determined by the Riemann zeros. If RH is true, M(x) = O(x^(1/2+ε)).

Connection to Hyperbolic Geometry

Selberg Trace Formula: For a compact hyperbolic surface X = Γ\ℍ², the trace formula relates the Laplacian spectrum (λₖ = 1/4 + rₖ²) to the lengths of closed geodesics (l_γ).
Spectral Gap ↔ Riemann Hypothesis:
  • Hyperbolic surface: λ₁ ≥ 3/16 (Friedman 2008)
  • Selberg conjecture: λ₁ ≥ 1/4
  • Riemann zeta: Re(ρ) = 1/2 ↔ λ = 1/4 + r² ≥ 1/4
  • The spectral gap is the geometric analog of RH
Hilbert-Pólya Conjecture: There exists a Hermitian operator H whose eigenvalues are the imaginary parts of the Riemann zeros. The Berry-Keating Hamiltonian H = xp is the leading candidate.
Recent Breakthrough (2024-2026): OpenAI proved a quasi-Riemann Hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions: all zeros have Re(s) ≤ 7/8. This excludes Siegel zeros and gives power-saving error terms in the prime number theorem for arithmetic progressions.
