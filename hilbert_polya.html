The Hilbert-Pólya Conjecture

The Riemann Hypothesis states that all non-trivial zeros of ζ(s) lie on the critical line Re(s) = 1/2. The Hilbert-Pólya conjecture proposes that these zeros are the eigenvalues of some Hermitian operator H — making the hypothesis a statement about the spectrum of a quantum mechanical system.

H|ψₖ⟩ = γₖ|ψₖ⟩ where γₖ are the Riemann zeros

GUE Statistics

The spacing distribution of Riemann zeros matches the Gaussian Unitary Ensemble (GUE) of random matrix theory. This means the zeros behave like energy levels of a chaotic quantum system — the same statistics describe heavy atomic nuclei and quantum billiards.

R₂(w) = 1 - (sin(πw)/(πw))² (GUE pair correlation)

Selberg Trace Formula

For hyperbolic surfaces, the Selberg trace formula connects Laplacian eigenvalues to closed geodesic lengths. The Riemann zeros play the role of "geodesic lengths" in the prime geodesic theorem, with the Möbius function extracting primitive contributions in both contexts.

Berry-Keating Hamiltonian

The classical Hamiltonian H = xp generates dilations. Under a non-standard quantization, its spectrum is conjectured to match the Riemann zeros. This connects the scaling symmetry of prime numbers to quantum mechanics.

H = xp → eigenvalues ≈ Riemann zeros
